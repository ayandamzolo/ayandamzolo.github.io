Conversations that do not waste anyone's time

A WhatsApp bot for an internet provider that re-engages customers with unpaid orders, and hands over to a human when it cannot help.

  • Internet provider
  • Sole author, 127 commits
  • Laravel, WhatsApp Cloud API, Pest

The bot's second job is the hard one

An order sits unpaid. Somebody meant to pay it and then their day happened. A message asking about that order is useful. A message that promises help nobody is at a desk to give is not, and it costs more trust than the nudge was ever going to earn.

So the bot has two jobs. It restarts the conversation about an unpaid order, and it gets out of the way the moment it cannot help. Almost every fix I made in it is about the second job: not leaving a person hanging.

The kinds of fix the commit log is full of

  • Do not say "shortly" to someone outside working hours. Shortly is a promise, and after hours it is one nothing in the system can keep.
  • One support number and one set of hours, in one place, so two parts of the bot cannot quote different ones. And never offer a team that is offline.
  • Answer a double booked installation instead of going silent. Someone telling you two technicians are arriving on the same day deserves a reply, even when the reply is only that a person is taking it from here.
  • Do not reply "didn't catch that" to someone who has just typed a date. If the parser is the thing that failed, the customer is not the one who should be told they were unclear.
  • Hold escalations raised outside working hours, and tell the person when we reopen. The escalation still goes through. It waits somewhere visible instead of landing in an empty room.
  • Stop sending courtesy nudges the provider is guaranteed to reject. A message that cannot be delivered is not a courtesy. It is a failed send that somebody has to explain later.

What the tests protect

The tests are not about the conversation that goes well. They cover the shapes real traffic turns up in, which is where a bot starts talking nonsense at people.

  • Webhooks arriving out of order, so an older status cannot overwrite a newer one
  • Replies landing on conversations that have already been closed
  • Messages from senders the system has never seen before
  • The bot's own records disagreeing with the provider's, which is the case that decides whether we trust our row or theirs

Each of those has a version where the customer is the one who pays for it: a stale status that makes us nudge someone who already paid, or a reply that vanishes because the conversation it belongs to was closed a minute earlier.

Messaged, replied, paid

Behind the bot is a funnel: how many customers were messaged, how many replied, how many paid. It exists so that "is this campaign worth sending at all" has an answer that is not somebody's impression of one. A nudge that moves nobody from replied to paid is not neutral. It is noise arriving on a person's phone, and the funnel is what makes that visible enough to stop.
