Finding what went quiet

When replies stop arriving, nothing errors. The work simply sits. This is the part I am best at, and it is a method rather than a talent.

  • All three platforms
  • MySQL forensics across tenant databases
  • CloudWatch
  • Scripts committed beside the fix

Nothing throws, so nothing pages

The failures I get called into are not the ones that turn something red. A reply that never arrives raises no exception. There is no stack trace, because no code ran. The record sits in the state it was left in, looking exactly like a record that is merely still in progress, and the first person to notice is usually the customer asking why nothing has happened.

That shape of failure is why I do not start by fixing anything. Whatever put a record in the wrong state has already been running unobserved for a while, and the worst available outcome is a second wrong write layered on top of the first one, made in a hurry by me.

The first pass writes nothing

I keep the investigation as a numbered series of scripts. The numbers are not decoration: they record the order the questions have to be answered in, so whoever runs them next does not have to reconstruct my reasoning before they can start. The early ones only read.

  • Every query is anchored on the business reference, never on a row identifier I remember. A recalled identifier is memory, not evidence, and memory is what I am here to check.
  • Nothing writes until the reads have been run and understood. Not read, not skimmed: run, and understood.
  • Each query stands alone, so any one of them can be rerun on its own without replaying the ones before it.

That last rule earns its keep at an awkward hour. An investigation that only works as one long session has to be restarted from the top every time something interrupts it, and the person restarting it is tired.

Repair values come from the system, not from me

When a write is finally justified, the values in it are copied out of the upstream system's own stored response. Not from a screenshot, not from what somebody remembers the figure being, and not from what it obviously should be. If the correct value cannot be pointed at where it is stored, then the repair is a guess, and a confident wrong figure is worse than an empty field.

The same applies to time. The write uses the stored submission timestamp, not the current one. Stamping it with now records the moment I noticed, which is the one piece of information nobody needs, and it overwrites the only evidence of the real sequence of events.

A dashboard is not a diagnosis

Most of the useful detail is one layer below where the alerting looks. From a dashboard, a full disk is one red number. A level down it is one table holding almost all of it, and then there is something to actually fix rather than something to escalate.

While that is going on I stay on the line. Stakeholders get updated the whole way through rather than silence followed by a resolution, because people can plan around a problem they can see, and cannot plan around one they have only been told is being looked at.

Then the post-mortem gets written

The repair is not the end of the work. The next person to meet this failure deserves the map: what went quiet, how it was found, which queries answered which question, and what to run first next time. Knowledge that only lives in my head is a single point of failure, and I am not always the one on duty.

Evidence. Eight production post-mortems and the vendor cutover runbooks are committed in the repository rather than living in my head, alongside the numbered investigation scripts. The method has been run across a lending platform, an internet provider and a travel booking platform.
